The Five Solas

Five hundred years ago, the pi­on­eers of our Reformed faith laid out five prin­ci­ples. We call them the Five Solas, because in the or­ig­in­al Latin, the name of each prin­ci­ple began with the word Sola or a form of it, which means "Alone". Here they are:

Sola Scriptura—Scripture Alone

The Bible, being the Word of God (2 Peter 1:19-21), is the ul­ti­mate au­thor­ity (Luke 4:4), not the trad­it­ions of the church or the opin­ions of men (Matthew 4:4, John 17:17).

Sola Gratia—Grace Alone

Salvation is by God's grace alone (Ephesians 2:4-9). Get­ting to hea­ven does not de­pend upon how well we live. God chose to save Jacob and re­ject Esau be­fore they were born (Romans 9:11-13).

Sola Fide—Faith Alone

We are just­i­fied by faith al­one, a­part from our works (Romans 3:28, Philippians 1:29). Through faith a­lone we re­ceive Christ and His per­fect right­eous­ness, and are there­by grant­ed e­ter­nal par­don.

Solus Christus—Christ Alone

God saves us be­cause of Jesus Christ a­lone, and not be­cause of any­thing we do (Ephesians 2:8-10). Just as all men are born sin­ners be­cause of Adam's sin, so all be­lie­vers re­ceive e­ter­nal par­don be­cause of Christ's blood a­tone­ment on the cross. Eve­ry­thing we do is sim­ply a re­sponse of gra­ti­tude to God for sav­ing us through Christ (Romans 1:21, Joshua 24:14).

Soli Deo Gloria—For the Glory of God Alone

God a­lone de­serves all the glo­ry and the praise for the sal­va­tion of His peo­ple (Philippians 2:13). If man's sal­va­tion de­pends ul­ti­mate­ly upon man's choice, then man de­serves the glory and praise. We bring praise to God by serv­ing Him ac­cord­ing to His Word and by the power of His Spi­rit (Ephesians 5:8-9). This is the true meaning of life.
