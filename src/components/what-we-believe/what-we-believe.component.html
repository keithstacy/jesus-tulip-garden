What We Believe

Why do we need God?

Humanity has a problem. It's a a problem that we caused for our­selves, but we can't fix it our­selves—it is be­yond our power to do so.

You see, our first ancest­ors were created to be perfect beings, but they still had a choice—they could obey God the Creator, or they could reject Him. To obey Him would have meant having a perfect life, and a life that would last forever. But they made the terr­ible choice to reject God, and when they did that, they brought the sent­ence of death upon them­selves. They would be mortal, and they would be unable to take back the choice they made. What's worse, they passed that sent­ence of death on to all of us who have come after them.

So why can't we fix the prob­lem? Can't we just de­cide to obey God? Can't we set things right on our own? Sadly, no. Part of the mort­al­ity we inherit makes it so that we will always—always—sin against God. It's in­ev­it­able that we will turn away from God. It is in­ev­it­able that we will sin. And when we sin, we re­in­force the death sent­ence we in­her­ited. We prove to all of time and e­ter­ni­ty that we are what God says we are—sin­ners de­serv­ing of e­ter­nal pun­ish­ment.

Will it help if we do more good deeds than evil ones? So many people seem to think so. But God's answer is a resounding No. The Lord Jesus Christ said, "Therefore you shall be perfect, just as your Father in heaven is perfect (Matthew 5:48)." It's not about get­ting to be good e­nough for God. You can never, ever be­come good e­nough for God. It is too late. You are al­rea­dy lost. That's ter­ri­ble news! But God has an ans­wer.

God says that sin must be paid for. You can pay for your own sin by ex­ist­ing in e­ter­nal pun­ish­ment, or you can let God pay for them. Is that for real? Can God real­ly pay for our sin and take away the pun­ish­ment we de­serve? Yes! You see, Jesus Christ, the Son of God, is no mere mort­al who is sad­dled with the same pun­ish­ment that we are. He is, rather, God Him­self, fused into hu­man­ity, born into un­stained, sin­less human flesh. And when He died upon the cross there in Jeru­salem, he took upon Him­self the ter­ri­ble pun­ish­ment we de­serve. And He proved it to be so by rais­ing Him­self up from death!

So your choice now is this: will you con­tin­ue on your current course toward e­ter­nal de­struc­tion, or will you turn to God and call on Him to save you from your sin? You can today, right where you are, ask Jesus to rescue you from the pun­ish­ment you de­serve for your re­jec­tion of God. You can be born into God's fam­ily. You can ex­per­i­ence the am­a­zing free­dom from being trap­ped by your past. You can ex­per­i­ence a life that is so won­der­ful, so full of joy, that no­thing you ever ex­per­i­enced be­fore can match it.

If you take this step today, please con­tact us and let us know. We can help you learn how to con­tin­ue in free­dom, joy, and peace with God.

What We Believe About
The Bible

Is this book really from God?

Throughout the centuries, Christians have held that the Bible is God's revealed Word, given through proph­ets and apostles so that human­ity can come to know God person­ally and re­ceive eter­nal life. We at St. Paul's Church em­brace this truth whole­heart­edly, and we teach it as the one and only source of truth for life and hope for all people every­where. We encour­age our members to read it every day, and we get together often to study the Bible to deepen our faith and our under­stand­ing of who God is. One of the great creeds of the Church, the Belgic Con­fes­sion, says,

We con­fess that this Word of God was not sent nor de­liv­ered by the will of man, but that men spake from God, being moved by the Holy Spir­it, as the apo­stle Peter says; and that af­ter­wards God, from a spe­cial care which He has for us and our sal­va­tion, com­mand­ed His ser­vants, the proph­ets and a­post­les, to com­mit His re­vealed word to writing; and He Him­self wrote with His own fing­er the two tab­les of the law. There­fore we call such writ­ings holy and div­ine Scrip­tures.

This is the core of what we believe about the Bible:*

  1. God, who is Him­self Truth and speaks truth only, has in­spired Holy Scrip­ture in order thereby to reveal Him­self to lost man­kind through Jesus Christ as Cre­at­or and Lord, Re­deem­er and Judge. Holy Scrip­ture is God’s wit­ness to Him­self.
  2. Holy Scrip­ture, being God’s own Word, written by men pre­pared and super­intend­ed by His Spirit, is of in­fal­lible divine auth­ority in all mat­ters upon which it touch­es: It is to be be­lieved, as God’s in­struc­tion, in all that it af­firms; obey­ed, as God’s command, in all that it re­quires; em­braced, as God’s pledge, in all that it pro­mis­es.
  3. The Holy Spirit, Scrip­ture’s divine Author, both auth­ent­i­cates it to us by His in­ward wit­ness and opens our minds to under­stand its mean­ing.
  4. Being whol­ly and verb­al­ly God-given, Scrip­ture is without error or fault in all its teach­ing, no less in what it states about God’s acts in cre­a­tion, about the events of world history, and about its own lit­er­ary or­i­gins under God, than in its wit­ness to God’s saving grace in in­div­id­ual lives.
  5. The auth­or­ity of Scrip­ture is in­esc­ap­a­bly im­pair­ed if this tot­al div­ine in­err­an­cy is in any way lim­i­ted or dis­re­gard­ed, or made rel­avtive to a view of truth con­tra­ry to the Bible’s own; and such laps­es bring ser­i­ous loss to both the in­div­id­ual and the Church.

Of course, this just scratch­es the sur­face of the mean­ing of the Bible. There is so much more to know about this life-giving book. If you want to ex­per­i­ence a life bey­ond any­thing you could have ever im­ag­ined, please contact us. We can show you who God real­ly is, and help you be­gin a new life—a beau­ti­ful, mean­ing­ful life—one that will last for­ev­er.

What We Believe About
Faith

What is faith?

Let's start by describing what it isn't. People talk about faith being a set of teach­ings, or a mere­ly hu­man be­lief in some­thing or some­one. But that's not what we're talk­ing about here. Auth­entic faith in God is not get­ting your­self psyched up over some­thing. It isn't mere­ly a set of abs­tract re­lig­ious be­liefs to which you give some int­el­lect­ual a­gree­ment, eith­er.

You can't do it your­self

Just ac­cept­ing a doc­trine or dog­ma, how­ever valid, isn't auth­entic faith. Many of us who have grown up in church­es oft­en take some teach­ing to be true. And e­ven those who nev­er went to church may have some opin­ion that is con­sist­ent with the teach­ing of Scrip­ture. Now right op­in­ions and ac­cept­ance of sound doc­trine may point us in the right di­rec­tion, but that simply does not go far e­nough.

And hav­ing emo­tion­al ex­per­i­en­ces isn't what real faith is about. A per­son to whom God has given real faith may have mo­ments of high emo­tion—what we some­times call "mount­ain­top" ex­per­i­en­ces—but e­mo­tions can fool you. You may be ut­ter­ly lost and with­out God in the world, while enjoy­ing hap­pi­ness and think­ing that ev­er­y­thing's just fine, right up un­til the day you die.

This is where God has to come in.

The real thing

Real faith—the faith that saves you from e­ter­nal ruin—is the pro­found su­per­nat­ur­al con­fid­ence that God is real. Hebrews 11:1 says, "Now faith is the sub­stance of things hoped for, the evi­dence of things not seen." In other words, real faith—the faith that God offers you as a gift—is the con­firm­ation of the things that God pro­mises to those who will open them­selves up to Him.

Faith is the proof of the things we can't yet see. Faith per­ceives the real­ity of the things that aren't re­vealed to the physical senses. And be­cause this is a su­per­nat­ural con­fid­ence, this faith is some­thing that God Him­self must give you. You can't just decide to agree with it and make it so. As the great Bible scholar John Cal­vin wrote, "[T]he know­ledge of faith con­sists more of cert­ain­ty than dis­cern­ment." It isn't some­thing you can fig­ure out. It is too big for your int­el­lect, your soul, your heart.

You see, it is God that has to do the in­it­ia­ting of your faith, be­cause you are ir­rep­ar­a­bly bro­ken. Your ab­i­li­ty to per­ceive God was de­stroy­ed from the mo­ment you came to be. And God knows—has known from e­ter­nity past—who will be­lieve and who won't. If you are read­ing this now, God may be call­ing to you to come to Him.

Do you want to seek God? If you do, don't stop here. Get a Bible, or go online to find it. Start reading it—the Gos­pel of John is a good place to start. And then get a hold of us. We'd love to en­cour­age you in your seek­ing after God.



What We Believe About
God's Grace

What is grace?

If you've nev­er been a­round church­es in your life, you may won­der, "What is this 'grace' I hear a­bout?" Per­haps you have been ex­posed to Christian churches, but you aren't quite sure what this is sup­posed to mean. So let's be­gin at the be­gin­ning.

The dic­tion­ary has a lot of dif­fer­ent mean­ings for the word "grace"— ev­er­y­thing from "sim­ple el­e­gance or re­fine­ment of move­ment" to "a short prayer of thanks said be­fore or aft­er a meal." But the mean­ing we're talk­ing ab­out is this: the grace of God is His free, un­de­serv­ed fa­vor, which He man­i­fests in sav­ing lost, con­demn­ed hu­man be­ings from their re­bel­lion a­gainst Him.

Let's break it down: The first asp­ect of God's grace is that it is free. If you fill a gro­cery cart full of goods and take it to the check­out aisle, what will you ex­pect when the check­er has scanned all your food? You're go­ing to pay for those it­ems, right? But what if the check­er hand­ed you the re­ceipt, and said, "No charge!" Would­n't you be am­azed? Would­n't you be grate­ful? Esp­e­cial­ly if you did­n't have the mon­ey to pay for it? I would.

Now think ab­out this: some peo­ple think that to get to hea­ven, you have to work hard, do more good deeds than bad, ob­ey the rules, and other­wise be a "good" per­son. In o­ther words, you have to pay for hea­ven with how you live your life. But what if you don't have en­ough? What if you can't af­ford it? What if the price is too high?

The sec­ond asp­ect of God's grace is that it is un­de­serv­ed. Not only do you not have e­nough good­ness to get into hea­ven, you don't even de­serve it. "But why not?" I can hear some­one ask. You see, all those good deeds you've been stock­pil­ing to pay for your tick­et into God's pre­sence—they are all count­er­feit. Pho­ny. Use­less. But how can that be? God warns us in the Bible,

But we are all like an unclean thing,
And all our righteousnesses are like filthy rags;
We all fade as a leaf,
And our iniquities, like the wind,
Have taken us away.

—Isaiah 64:6 (NKJV)

All those good deeds we think we're do­ing? All that right liv­ing? In God's eyes, it's filth.

That's right. God thinks no more of your "good" be­hav­ior than he does of your sin­ful be­hav­ior. Why? Be­cause all of hu­man­ity—each and ev­ery one of us who has ev­er been born or ev­er will be born—is cor­rupt­ed by sin. And cor­rup­tion isn't some­thing you can patch, or smooth over, or cov­er with a lick of paint. There's only one thing to do with it—de­stroy it.

But God has a plan.

And that plan is some­thing we sim­ply don't de­serve. We de­serve to be de­stroyed. But God made a way to de­stroy our sin, but save us. And if you will beg God to put you into His plan...you can be saved. Res­cued. Freed from your sin. Keep read­ing—you'll learn what the Lord Jesus Christ did to put God's plan into mo­tion.



What We Believe About
Jesus Christ

Who is Jesus?

Jesus Christ is God Him­self. There's a lot more about this that should be said, but that is the start­ing point of it all. If you un­der­stand that, great! God stands ready to give you real faith (and maybe you have al­ready re­ceived this am­az­ing gift!) If you don't have it, you need to read on.

C.S. Lewis, the auth­or of The Chron­icles of Nar­nia, once wrote,

I am try­ing here to pre­vent any­one say­ing the real­ly fool­ish thing that peo­ple often say about Him: I'm ready to ac­cept Jesus as a great mor­al teach­er, but I don't ac­cept his claim to be God. That is the one thing we must not say. A man who was mere­ly a man and said the sort of things Jesus said would not be a great mor­al teach­er. He would eith­er be a lun­a­tic—on the lev­el with the man who says he is a poached egg—or else he would be the Dev­il of Hell. You must make your choice. Eith­er this man was, and is, the Son of God, or else a mad­man or some­thing worse. You can shut him up for a fool, you can spit at him and kill him as a de­mon or you can fall at his feet and call him Lord and God, but let us not come with any pa­tron­iz­ing non­sense about his being a great human teach­er. He has not left that open to us. He did not in­tend to.

Jesus, the Man born in Beth­le­hem two thou­sand years ago, who lived a sin­less life and taught in anc­ient Is­rael for three years, is the Son of God. That means He is the God the Son, the sec­ond Per­son of the Tri­une God, fused in­to hu­man­i­ty and born of the vir­gin Mary. He came to make it pos­si­ble for us sin­ful, God-re­ject­ing peo­ple to be changed—born spir­it­ual­ly into a new, clean, e­ter­nal life. When He died on the cross at Jeru­salem, He took upon Him­self the aw­ful, ter­ri­fy­ing pun­ish­ment that we de­serve, so that when any­one comes to Him and asks Him to res­cue us from hell, he sub­sti­tutes his own death for ours. He pays the pen­al­ty for us. He makes us fit to spend a beaut­i­ful, glor­i­ous e­ter­ni­ty with Him.

How do we know that Jesus really is the Son of God?
First, He act­ual­ly claimed to be the Son of God. John 10:24 begins,

Then the Jews sur­round­ed Him and said to Him, “How long do You keep us in doubt? If You are the Christ, tell us plain­ly.” Jesus ans­wer­ed them, “I told you, and you do not be­lieve. The works that I do in My Fa­ther’s name, they bear wit­ness of Me. But you do not be­lieve, be­cause you are not of My sheep, as I said to you. My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they fol­low Me. And I give them e­ter­nal life, and they shall never per­ish; neith­er shall any­one snatch them out of My hand. My Fa­ther, who has given them to Me, is great­er than all; and no one is able to snatch them out of My Fa­ther’s hand. I and My Father are one.”

A little farther down, the Scripture reads,

Then the Jews took up stones again to stone Him. Jesus ans­wer­ed them, “Many good works I have shown you from My Fa­ther. For which of those works do you stone Me?” The Jews ans­wer­ed Him, saying, “For a good work we do not stone You, but for blas­phem­y, and be­cause You, being a Man, make Your­self God.” Jesus ans­wer­ed them, “Is it not writ­ten in your law, ‘I said, “You are gods” ’? If He called them gods, to whom the word of God came (and the Scrip­ture can­not be bro­ken), do you say of Him whom the Father sanct­i­fied and sent into the world, ‘You are blas­pheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’? If I do not do the works of My Fa­ther, do not be­lieve Me; but if I do, though you do not be­lieve Me, be­lieve the works, that you may know and be­lieve that the Fa­ther is in Me, and I in Him.”

Those around Him rec­og­nized that He was mak­ing that bold claim, and they hat­ed Him for it. And He did­n't deny it—He stood firm in mak­ing that claim. And there are many other pla­ces in the Gos­pels where He as­sert­ed his auth­ent­ic claim to be the Son of God.

Sec­ond, He proved it again and again by migh­ty mir­a­cles like heal­ing the sick, re­stor­ing sight to the blind, giv­ing hear­ing to the deaf, mak­ing the lame walk, cast­ing out de­mons, raising the dead. Over and over again Jesus demonstrated his awesome power, leaving those who followed Him with no doubt—this Man Jesus was truly God in the flesh. There were so many mir­a­cles that the Gospel writer declares, "And tru­ly Jesus did many oth­er signs in the pre­sence of His dis­ci­ples, which are not writ­ten in this book; but these are writ­ten that you may be­lieve that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that be­liev­ing you may have life in His name."

Third, He raised Him­self from death after his cru­ci­fix­ion to prove His div­ine pow­er and His right to save us from hell. The first clue is that Jesus claim­ed the pow­er to raise Him­self from the dead. In John 10:17-18, Jesus says, "There­fore My Fa­ther loves Me, be­cause I lay down My life that I may take it again. No one takes it from Me, but I lay it down of My­self. I have pow­er to lay it down, and I have pow­er to take it again." This is Our Faith
In a Nutshell

Five hundred years ago, the pi­on­eers of our faith laid out five prin­ci­ples. We call them the Five Solas, because in the or­ig­in­al Latin, the name of each prin­ci­ple began with the word Sola or a form of it, which means "Alone". Here they are:

Sola Scriptura—Scripture Alone

The Bible, being the Word of God (2 Peter 1:19-21), is the ul­ti­mate au­thor­ity (Luke 4:4), not the trad­it­ions of the church or the opin­ions of men (Matthew 4:4, John 17:17).

Sola Gratia—Grace Alone

Salvation is by God's grace alone (Ephesians 2:4-9). Get­ting to hea­ven does not de­pend upon how well we live. God chose to save Jacob and re­ject Esau be­fore they were born (Romans 9:11-13).

Sola Fide—Faith Alone

We are just­i­fied by faith al­one, a­part from our works (Romans 3:28, Philippians 1:29). Through faith a­lone we re­ceive Christ and His per­fect right­eous­ness, and are there­by grant­ed e­ter­nal par­don.

Solus Christus—Christ Alone

God saves us be­cause of Jesus Christ a­lone, and not be­cause of any­thing we do (Ephesians 2:8-10). Just as all men are born sin­ners be­cause of Adam's sin, so all be­lie­vers re­ceive e­ter­nal par­don be­cause of Christ's blood a­tone­ment on the cross. Eve­ry­thing we do is sim­ply a re­sponse of gra­ti­tude to God for sav­ing us through Christ (Romans 1:21, Joshua 24:14).

Soli Deo Gloria—For the Glory of God Alone

God a­lone de­serves all the glo­ry and the praise for the sal­va­tion of His peo­ple (Philippians 2:13). If man's sal­va­tion de­pends ul­ti­mate­ly upon man's choice, then man de­serves the glory and praise. We bring praise to God by serv­ing Him ac­cord­ing to His Word and by the power of His Spi­rit (Ephesians 5:8-9). This is the true meaning of life.
