What We Believe

Why do we need God?

Humanity has a problem. It's a a problem that we caused for our­selves, but we can't fix it our­selves—it is be­yond our power to do so.

You see, our first ancest­ors were created to be perfect beings, but they still had a choice—they could obey God the Creator, or they could reject Him. To obey him would have meant having a perfect life, and a life that would last forever. But they made the terr­ible choice to reject God, and when they did that, they brought the sent­ence of death upon them­selves. They would be mortal, and they would be unable to take back the choice they made. What's worse, they passed that sent­ence of death on to all of us who have come after them.

So why can't we fix the prob­lem? Can't we just de­cide to obey God? Can't we set things right on our own? Sadly, no. Part of the mort­al­ity we inherit makes it so that we will always—always—sin against God. It's in­ev­it­able that we will turn away from God. It is in­ev­it­able that we will sin. And when we sin, we re­in­force the death sent­ence we in­her­ited. We prove to all of time and e­ter­ni­ty that we are what God says we are—sin­ners de­serv­ing of e­ter­nal pun­ish­ment.

Will it help if we do more good deeds than evil ones? So many people seem to think so. But God's answer is a resounding No. The Lord Jesus Christ said, "Therefore you shall be perfect, just as your Father in heaven is perfect (Matthew 5:48)." It's not about get­ting to be good e­nough for God. You can never, ever be­come good e­nough for God. It is too late. You are al­rea­dy lost. That's ter­ri­ble news! But God has an ans­wer.

God says that sin must be paid for. You can pay for your own sin by ex­ist­ing in e­ter­nal pun­ish­ment, or you can let God pay for them. Is that for real? Can God real­ly pay for our sin and take away the pun­ish­ment we de­serve? Yes! You see, Jesus Christ, the Son of God, is no mere mort­al who is sad­dled with the same pun­ish­ment that we are. He is, rather, God Him­self, fused into hu­man­ity, born into un­stained, sin­less human flesh. And when He died upon the cross there in Jeru­salem, he took upon Him­self the ter­ri­ble pun­ish­ment we de­serve. And He proved it to be so by rais­ing Him­self up from death!

So your choice now is this: will you con­tin­ue on your current course toward e­ter­nal de­struc­tion, or will you turn to God and call on Him to save you from your sin? You can today, right where you are, ask Jesus to rescue you from the pun­ish­ment you de­serve for your re­jec­tion of God. You can be born into God's fam­ily. You can ex­per­i­ence the am­a­zing free­dom from being trap­ped by your past. You can ex­per­i­ence a life that is so won­der­ful, so full of joy, that no­thing you ever ex­per­i­enced be­fore can match it.

If you take this step today, please con­tact us and let us know. We can help you learn how to con­tin­ue in free­dom, joy, and peace with God.

Let's talk about what we believe!

The things we be­lieve go all the way back to the be­gin­nings of his­tory. Our be­liefs are based on the time­less truth of the Bible, the re­vealed writ­ten word of God Him­self. The Bible tells us that this Word of God can­not be broken (John 10:35). Be­cause of this, the prom­is­es of God were ful­fil­led not just through­out hi­story (Joshua 23:14), but also in Jesus Christ (2 Corinthians 1:20). And there's so much more about the Bible—like how God has im­bued it with a spir­it­ual power (Hebrews 4:12) that can open your eyes to the re­al­ity of some­thing—Some­one—great­er than your­self. Some­one who can turn you into a new per­son. Some­one who can make the life you have now seem like no­thing com­pared to what He of­fers you.


Our faith, in a nutshell

Five hundred years ago, the pi­on­eers of our faith laid out five prin­ci­ples. We call them the Five Solas, because in the or­ig­in­al Latin, the name of each prin­ci­ple began with the word Sola or a form of it, which means "Alone". Here they are:

Sola Scriptura—Scripture Alone

The Bible, being the Word of God (2 Peter 1:19-21), is the ul­ti­mate au­thor­ity (Luke 4:4), not the trad­it­ions of the church or the opin­ions of men (Matthew 4:4, John 17:17).

Sola Gratia—Grace Alone

Salvation is by God's grace alone (Ephesians 2:4-9). Get­ting to hea­ven does not de­pend upon how well we live. God chose to save Jacob and re­ject Esau be­fore they were born (Romans 9:11-13).

Sola Fide—Faith Alone

We are just­i­fied by faith al­one, a­part from our works (Romans 3:28, Philippians 1:29). Through faith a­lone we re­ceive Christ and His per­fect right­eous­ness, and are there­by grant­ed e­ter­nal par­don.

Solus Christus—Christ Alone

God saves us be­cause of Jesus Christ a­lone, and not be­cause of any­thing we do (Ephesians 2:8-10). Just as all men are born sin­ners be­cause of Adam's sin, so all be­lie­vers re­ceive e­ter­nal par­don be­cause of Christ's blood a­tone­ment on the cross. Eve­ry­thing we do is sim­ply a re­sponse of gra­ti­tude to God for sav­ing us through Christ (Romans 1:21, Joshua 24:14).

Soli Deo Gloria—For the Glory of God Alone

God a­lone de­serves all the glo­ry and the praise for the sal­va­tion of His peo­ple (Philippians 2:13). If man's sal­va­tion de­pends ul­ti­mate­ly upon man's choice, then man de­serves the glory and praise. We bring praise to God by serv­ing Him ac­cord­ing to His Word and by the power of His Spi­rit (Ephesians 5:8-9). This is the true meaning of life.

What we believe about giving
In the Old Testament God com­mand­ed that a tithe, ten per­cent, of all that He has prov­id­ent­ial­ly blessed us with be giv­en back to God. We read in Leviticus 27:32, "con­cern­ing the tithe of the herd or the flock, of what­ever pas­ses un­der the rod, the tenth one shall be holy to the LORD," (see also Numbers 18:26, Deuteronomy 14:24, 2 Chronicles 31:5). Even with the idea of of­fer­ing the first fruits (Leviticus 2:12) giv­ing must be done trust­ing God will pro­vide (Proverbs 3:9-10). The tithe was for the sup­port of the Tab­ern­acle, and la­ter, the Tem­ple and the work of min­is­try. It was also for the up­keep and re­pair of the tem­ple (Nehemiah 10:38, Haggai 1:4-11). It was also used to meet the needs of for­eign­ers, orph­ans and wid­ows (Deuteronomy 26:12-13). Now since the New Test­am­ent builds on and clar­if­ies the Old Test­am­ent, what does our un­change­able God (Malachi 3:6) de­sire of us now as His church? In the NT we still see the com­mand to sup­port the min­is­try of the word of God (1 Timothy 5:18), widows (1 Timothy 5:9-10) and the poor (Romans 15:26). While Christ ful­fills the Law of Moses, we still are to obey the moral law out of thank­ful­ness. No­where do we see a com­mand that tith­ing is done. Christ even prais­es the wid­ow who gave her two mites (Luke 21:3-4). Why? Because of her heart at­ti­tude! Now that Christ, not with sil­ver or gold but His pre­cious blood has re­deemed us, we must now look at giv­ing back to the Lord with a new att­it­ude! "Not grudgingly or of necessity; for God loves a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7). Int­er­est­ing­ly enough, if God puts the burd­en on a heart, be­cause of abil­ity (1 Corinthians 16:2, 1 Peter 4:10), the New Test­am­ent even ex­pands the Old Test­am­ent com­mand tell­ing us to give in lib­er­al­ity (Romans 12:8) know­ing "it is more blessed to give than re­ceive" (Acts 20:35). Our pur­pose now, with e­ter­nal life in view, is to be rich to God stor­ing up treas­ures for the life to come (1 Timothy 6:17-19).
